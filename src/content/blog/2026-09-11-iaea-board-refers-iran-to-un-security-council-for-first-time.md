The board of the International Atomic Energy Agency has voted to refer Iran to the United Nations Security Council due to its failure to cooperate with an investigation into uranium traces found at undeclared sites. The resolution passed with twenty-three votes in favour, three against, eight abstentions, and one country that did not vote. This marks the first time in twenty years that Iran has been referred to the Security Council, which could now consider punitive measures such as sanctions and asset freezes.

Western officials suspect the uranium traces point to an undeclared nuclear weapons programme that may have continued until 2003, while Iran denies the allegations and maintains its activities are peaceful. Iranian officials angrily condemned the decision as politically motivated by Western governments. Analysts note that any Security Council measures could be blocked by veto powers Russia and China, and diplomats expect the referral may carry more symbolic than immediate practical weight.
