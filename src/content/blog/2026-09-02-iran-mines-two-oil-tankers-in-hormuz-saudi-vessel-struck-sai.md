Two oil tankers carrying Saudi crude, including a very large carrier identified as the SIDR, were struck in the Strait of Hormuz by what analysts believe were naval mines. The attacks resulted in the deaths of two Filipino crew members and damaged a second tanker, putting several million barrels of crude at risk. 

While Iran's Revolutionary Guard has not formally claimed responsibility, shipping-security analysts and the method and location point strongly toward Tehran amid ongoing hostilities. The attacks are part of an increasing pattern of targeting shipping tied to Gulf Arab states that Iran accuses of backing the US-Israeli campaign, marking one of the first confirmed civilian maritime casualties of the conflict.

Saudi Arabia has confirmed the incident, and global oil markets are already reacting to potential disruptions in the critical strait. Maritime security firms are urging tankers transiting the area to exercise heightened caution and consider rerouting.
