According to an opinion piece shared by the Council on Foreign Relations, Pakistan's army chief and de facto leader, Field Marshal Asim Munir, has raised the country's international standing over the past year and a half despite mounting domestic challenges. A recent diplomatic win cited in the piece is the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement, signed last week by Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkey, which dictates that an armed attack on any of the three nations will be treated as an attack on all three.

The piece describes a "Pakistan paradox," noting that the same nuclear-armed military that gives the country international relevance also holds back its political and economic modernization. The opinion warns that unless Munir can revive Pakistan's stagnant economy and calm its restive population, his diplomatic gains will likely prove evanescent, as neither diplomatic feats nor consolidated power offer solutions to domestic crises.
