A governance dispute has escalated at India's Tata Group following a September 17 Tata Sons board meeting. During the meeting, directors voted four-to-one to reappoint N Chandrasekaran as executive chairman for another five-year term starting next February and took initial steps toward a potential public listing of the holding company. Tata Trusts chairman Noel Tata cast the lone dissenting vote, and the Trusts subsequently moved to declare the board's decision void.

At the core of the disagreement is the company's Articles of Association. Tata Trusts argues that these articles require unanimous agreement among trust-nominated directors present at a meeting to validate a chairman's appointment. With only two such directors attending and Noel Tata voting against, Trusts loyalists assert the resolution was invalid from the start, despite another nominee director siding with the majority.

Nationalist Congress Party (Sharadchandra Pawar) chief Sharad Pawar has publicly backed Tata Trusts, urging that their institutional role be respected. Pawar noted that philanthropic trusts hold about two-thirds of Tata Sons' equity, funding institutions like the Tata Memorial Hospital, the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, and the Tata Institute of Social Sciences. He called for the dispute to be resolved through dialogue and adherence to established governance processes, citing past Supreme Court recognition of trust-nominated directors' special voting rights.
