The Madras High Court has launched a suo motu public interest case aimed at making sure caste-based names are actually removed from streets, roads and localities across Tamil Nadu.  The matter was taken up on Thursday by a First Division Bench headed by Chief Justice Sushrut Arvind Dharmadhikari and including Justice G.  Arul Murugan.  The proceedings are meant to enforce a series of directions that the court has issued over the years on the subject, many of which have reportedly seen uneven follow-through on the ground.  The bench has asked the state government to file a status report within four weeks.

That report must list which caste references have already been removed and which ones are still in place.  The court's move gives the issue a single monitored forum rather than leaving it to scattered petitions and local body decisions.  It comes only a day after another bench of the same High Court, sitting at Madurai, upheld a 2025 state government order setting out a procedure to rename streets, colonies and water bodies that carry caste or objectionable names.  That order, issued by the Municipal Administration and Water Supply Department, provides for replacing such names with neutral ones or names honouring people recognised for public service.  In the Madurai ruling the judges rejected arguments that the exercise was politically motivated and described the 21-day objection window as not unreasonable to contest.

The judges there observed that caste names should eventually fade away, and said some of the designations in use were too derogatory to be spoken in open court.  Examples of renamings cited in that case included replacing colony names such as Harijan Colony and Sweeper Colony with neutral street names honouring Thiruvalluvar and Bharathidasan.  Taken together, the two developments show the judiciary pressing the Vijay-led state government and local bodies to move from policy on paper to visible change on signboards and revenue records.  The four-week deadline means the government will have to present an audit of progress soon, which could expose gaps between municipal resolutions and actual renaming.  Caste-linked place names have long been a sensitive subject in Tamil Nadu, tied to debates over social justice, dignity and political identity.
