A Middle East security analyst argues that an Iran war will result in a heavily reduced United States military footprint in the region by the end of the conflict. The analyst states that achieving a strategic end to the war will require the US to agree to a different force structure and quality for its regional presence, rather than simply declaring a military conclusion.

This perspective is part of a wider debate among Middle East analysts over whether the costs of the 2026 conflict will push Washington toward a lighter regional footprint and a greater reliance on burden-sharing and regional partners. The prediction follows a Washington Post report that the Pentagon is evaluating its Middle East military footprint.

The analyst frames the situation as the beginning of the end of the US presence east of NATO, suggesting a broader long-term retrenchment. No official US policy announcement has confirmed this prediction, and the debate is significant for Gulf states that have relied on the US security umbrella for decades.
