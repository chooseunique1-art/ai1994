U.S. Central Command reports that American forces have redirected 86 commercial vessels away from the Strait of Hormuz during the ongoing naval blockade against Iran. This is a sharp increase from the roughly five dozen vessels reported diverted just weeks prior. In addition to these redirections, military officials stated that U.S. forces have disabled three vessels that attempted to breach the blockade and boarded two others to check their cargo and compliance. 

The blockade was imposed in the spring after negotiations to end the war between the United States and Iran failed. The Strait of Hormuz has since become a militarized chokepoint where U.S. warships intercept, inspect, and turn back tankers and cargo ships suspected of violating the restrictions. As one of the main arteries for global oil and gas shipments, the rerouting has caused shipping companies to seek alternate routes or delay voyages to avoid seizure or damage, which has increased costs and delays in global energy markets. 

The blockade is also connected to a wider campaign of mine strikes and tanker attacks attributed to Iran against vessels linked to U.S.-allied Gulf states. CENTCOM publicizes these enforcement statistics to show the blockade's reach and warn operators against defying the restrictions. Analysts note that the rising number of interdictions points to a more assertive U.S. posture alongside ongoing attempts by vessels to evade the rules, keeping oil prices elevated due to fears of further supply disruptions from the region.
