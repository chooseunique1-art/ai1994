Kuwait and Pakistan signed a new joint defense and military cooperation agreement in Rawalpindi on August 27, 2026. The pact was signed by Pakistan's defense minister Khawaja Asif and his Kuwaiti counterpart. It covers military training, border management assistance, defense capacity building, intelligence sharing, and logistics support, and establishes an annual joint military committee to oversee it. 

While stopping short of collective-defense language, the agreement is described as a structured and formalization of ties rather than a symbolic gesture. The deal follows other recent Pakistani defense arrangements in the region, including a mutual defense agreement with Saudi Arabia, a role in the trilateral Saudi-Turkiye "Mecca Pact," troop and fighter squadron deployments to Saudi Arabia, and deepening training ties with Qatar. 

For Kuwait, a small and wealthy state with a modest military, formalizing military cooperation with a nuclear-armed regional power provides a hedge amid recent instability. The agreement reflects a broader pattern of Gulf capitals diversifying their security bets rather than relying on a single outside guarantor.
