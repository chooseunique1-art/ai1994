An opinion piece shared by the Council on Foreign Relations argues that Pakistan's army chief and de facto leader, Field Marshal Asim Munir, has raised the country's international standing while domestic challenges have mounted. The column points to Munir's recent diplomatic win, the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement signed by Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkey, which treats an armed attack on any of the three as an attack on all. 

Described as the "Pakistan paradox," the same nuclear-armed military that gives the country international relevance also holds back its political and economic modernization. The piece warns that unless Munir can revive the stagnant economy and calm the restive population, his diplomatic gains will prove evanescent, as neither diplomatic feats nor consolidated power solve domestic crises.
