Saudi Arabia is offering to sell oil from off the coast of Oman on a ship-to-ship basis, a possible sign the kingdom is shuttling more barrels through the Strait of Hormuz instead of transiting it directly. Under this method, tankers make a dark transit through the strait with identification systems off, then transfer cargo to waiting vessels off Oman for long-haul voyages. 

According to reporting, Saudi state shipping company Bahri has positioned 16 very large crude carriers off Oman, with three more heading there and two having loaded at the Ras Tanura export terminal in the prior week. The UAE's ADNOC and affiliated firms pioneered this two-tier approach earlier in the 2026 Iran war, and Saudi Arabia is also increasingly avoiding the Red Sea route due to renewed Houthi attacks. Around 150 ships are now floating off Oman awaiting transfers, up from roughly 40 in January 2026.

This strategy allows Gulf oil exporters to keep barrels flowing despite the risks of the war, though it adds cost and complexity to regional oil logistics. The shift occurs as Iran and Gulf states continue to dispute safe passage through the strait, including an August 8, 2026 Iranian missile strike on an ADNOC tanker.
