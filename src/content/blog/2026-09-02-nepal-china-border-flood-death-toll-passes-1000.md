The confirmed death toll from catastrophic flash flooding along the Nepal-China border has climbed past one thousand people, according to Nepal's foreign ministry. The disaster began in late August when heavy monsoon rainfall combined with a sudden glacial-related surge, sending a wall of water and debris down river valleys and wiping out entire villages within minutes. Approximately twenty-five hundred people were initially listed as missing, a number that is slowly narrowing as rescue teams search debris fields.

The United Nations has raised concern over the disproportionate toll on women and girls due to domestic responsibilities and mobility constraints. Cross-border coordination between Nepali and Chinese authorities has been required because the flood's origin and path span the shared frontier. Infrastructure damage includes bridges washed away, cutting off villages, while mountainous terrain has slowed relief delivery. 

Hospitals in Kathmandu have become gathering points for grieving families searching for missing loved ones. Nepali officials caution that the final death toll may continue to rise as search teams reach more remote areas.
