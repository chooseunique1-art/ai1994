On August 26, 2026, the Trump administration announced a worldwide pause on immigrant visa appointments at U.S. embassies and consulates. Affected applicants received email notifications that their scheduled interviews were being rescheduled. The State Department stated the pause is part of a new global training initiative designed to help consular officers screen applicants more consistently, including for the risk of dependence on U.S. public benefits. Officials framed the measures as a way to enhance domestic security.

The pause is distinct from an earlier policy that would have suspended immigrant visa processing for nationals of 75 countries, which a federal judge recently blocked after ruling it exceeded the Secretary of State's legal authority. Meanwhile, human rights organizations have condemned the broader immigration crackdown—which includes visa revocations, green card cancellations, and application rejections—as discriminatory, citing concerns over free speech, due-process rights, and racial or ethnic profiling.

It remains unclear how long the worldwide pause will last or how many applicants are affected. The move has created fresh uncertainty for prospective immigrants, along with their U.S.-based family members and employers, amid heightened scrutiny of the U.S. visa system.
