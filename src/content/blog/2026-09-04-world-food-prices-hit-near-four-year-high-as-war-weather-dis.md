The United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization reported that its global food price index climbed in August to its highest level since late 2022, averaging 133.3 points. This increase was driven by adverse weather and conflict-related disruptions in the Gulf and the Black Sea region that stoked concern over key staple crops.

Cereal, vegetable oil, and sugar prices all saw increases. Notably, sugar prices jumped nearly 12 percent due to weaker production forecasts from Brazil's center-south growing region, while the FAO also revised down its global cereal production forecast for 2026.

Economists warn that these sustained staple food cost increases disproportionately affect lower-income countries and households, raising the risk of food insecurity. International agencies have urged governments to avoid panic-driven export restrictions to prevent worsening shortages and price spikes.
