South Korea's presidential office announced that it is actively reviewing options, including possible military deployment, to safeguard freedom of navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, though no final decision has been made. The statement was issued in response to local media reports suggesting Seoul had already decided to send naval assets to the region. Reported options under consideration include deploying a P-8A Poseidon maritime patrol aircraft, sending the fast combat support ship ROKS Soyang, or contributing a mine detection and clearance unit. 

Seoul has reportedly discussed the scale and nature of any contribution with the United States, Britain, and France. Domestically, public opinion is split, with a poll earlier this year showing a majority opposed to sending warships to the strait and a smaller share in favor. Government officials stated that a final decision will depend on how the security situation around the strait evolves in the coming weeks.
