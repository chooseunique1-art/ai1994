Saudi Arabia's National Debt Management Center has approached international banks to discuss raising a fresh loan of roughly six to eight billion dollars. State oil giant Saudi Aramco is also pursuing similar borrowing conversations with its banking partners. The financing efforts come as the kingdom's economy absorbs the fallout from the war between the United States, Israel, and Iran, which has led to a steep second-quarter contraction driven largely by a sharp slump in the oil sector.

The conflict has repeatedly disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, raising insurance, freight, and import costs. The proposed five-year loan is part of a larger annual borrowing plan targeting roughly fifty-eight billion dollars in total issuance. 

Both the sovereign loan and Aramco's talks remain preliminary, with terms unsettled and no certainty that they will be finalized. Neighboring Gulf states have also reported hits to trade, tourism, and energy exports due to the ongoing instability.
