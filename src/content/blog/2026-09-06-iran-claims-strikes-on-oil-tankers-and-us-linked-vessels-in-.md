Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed responsibility for strikes on oil supertankers and US-linked vessels in the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian military commanders warned that attacks on US military assets and regional maritime traffic will intensify if American military strikes continue, following reported air strikes on military radar sites and coastal installations along the Persian Gulf.

Maritime safety agencies reported disruptions across key shipping lanes, causing commercial vessels to anchor off regional ports. Crude prices fluctuated near eighty dollars per barrel due to supply disruption fears, while regional nations expressed concern over commercial shipping and energy infrastructure safety. Defense analysts warned that the military actions threaten economic recoveries and energy stability, prompting international shipping federations to advise commercial operators to exercise extreme caution.

Neighboring Gulf states are pursuing diplomatic efforts to negotiate temporary safe navigation corridors and prevent full-scale conflict. Naval forces are stepping up surveillance operations, and energy-importing nations, including India, are monitoring developments. Meanwhile, unilateral drone and missile attacks in the Gulf continue to draw condemnation from international peacekeeping bodies and trade alliances.
