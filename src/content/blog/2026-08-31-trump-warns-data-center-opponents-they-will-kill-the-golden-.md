President Trump used a social media post to criticize the growing public backlash against data centers being built across the United States. He framed opposition to the facilities as a threat to the country's economic future, arguing that resisting communities risk becoming backwards and poor while those welcoming them will benefit from lower taxes and more jobs. Trump warned that blocking construction would "kill the Golden Goose" and suggested that rival power China would benefit most from any American retreat from data center expansion.

Opposition to data centers has emerged as an unexpectedly potent local issue ahead of the November midterm elections, cutting across party lines. Residents in numerous communities have raised concerns about rising electricity bills, constant noise from cooling systems, and heavy water usage associated with the server farms needed for the artificial intelligence boom. In response, some local and state officials from both parties have begun pushing back against new proposals or seeking stricter conditions.

Trump's administration has previously worked to expedite permitting for data center construction through an executive order aimed at speeding up the approval process. Meanwhile, major technology companies have pledged to generate more of their own power to reduce strain on local grids and address constituent complaints. This highlights a growing tension between the administration's enthusiasm for rapid AI infrastructure buildout and the bottom-up backlash from residents bearing the direct costs.
