President Donald Trump stated he is weighing a major decision on whether to escalate further against Tehran, describing choices that range from annihilating the Iranian government to holding back. The remarks come seven months into a conflict that began with a joint air campaign by the United States and Israel against Iran in late February, evolving into a battle for control of Gulf shipping lanes in the Strait of Hormuz.

Both sides have exchanged strikes on tankers moving through the waterway. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps reported hitting vessels it accuses of violating a restricted maritime route, while the United States has struck Iranian oil tankers in retaliation and to target oil exports that fund the IRGC. U.S. Central Command stated that several Iranian tankers have been permanently disabled or destroyed, while Iran's foreign ministry condemned the U.S. strikes as a war crime.

The standoff has caused commercial vessels to avoid the strait or attempt risky ship-to-ship transfers. India, heavily dependent on energy imports transiting the Gulf, is closely monitoring the situation, and regional actors like Saudi Arabia have increased defensive measures. Analysts note that Trump's public deliberation suggests Washington views the current tanker strikes as insufficient, raising the risk of wider confrontation.
