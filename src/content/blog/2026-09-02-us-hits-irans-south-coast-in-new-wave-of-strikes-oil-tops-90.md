A new wave of American military strikes hit Iran's southern coastline this week, causing explosions and fires at multiple points, including Bandar Abbas, Qeshm Island, Chabahar, Konarak, Jask, Sirik, and Minab. US Central Command stated the operation targeted Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps infrastructure and personnel in response to attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian state-linked accounts claimed thousands of target points were hit, while eyewitnesses and video in Bandar Abbas showed fires burning near port facilities and a major Iranian naval base. Local reporting also noted strikes near the Chabahar port.

In response to the escalation, benchmark crude prices jumped past ninety dollars a barrel as traders feared prolonged disruption to Gulf shipping lanes, with analysts discussing the possibility of triple-digit prices. Iran's leadership stated the strikes are an act of aggression that will not go unanswered, though diplomatic channels are still exploring off-ramps. The conflict's recent pattern involves intense exchanges followed by pauses, creating renewed uncertainty for countries and companies dependent on Gulf oil and shipping regarding fuel prices, tanker routing, and insurance costs.
