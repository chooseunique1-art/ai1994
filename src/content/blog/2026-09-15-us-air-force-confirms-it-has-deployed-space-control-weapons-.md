The United States has publicly acknowledged for the first time that it has deployed space control weapons into Earth's orbit. US Air Force Secretary Troy Meink stated at the Air, Space and Cyber Conference that the Space Force possesses these on-orbit weapons to defend the Joint Force against hostile adversary action, aiming the message at rivals China and Russia. Meink did not disclose the number of weapons, when they were placed in orbit, or their specific form.

Military space policy experts noted that using the word weapons rather than vaguer references marks the first explicit US confirmation of a designed weapon stationed in space. Analysts believe the systems are likely non-kinetic tools, such as electronic-warfare platforms or communications jammers, rather than kinetic weapons that physically destroy satellites, to avoid generating hazardous debris. The 1967 Outer Space Treaty bans nuclear weapons and weapons of mass destruction in orbit, but does not prohibit conventional weapons or counterspace systems like jammers.

China's foreign ministry criticized the disclosure and called on Washington to halt preparations for war in orbit, while the Kremlin called for space to remain free of any weapons. Researchers have previously documented that China and Russia also possess or are developing counterspace weapons. While an expert noted the US disclosure does not fundamentally alter the technical balance, it risks normalising the weaponisation of space and could prompt other nations to declare their own orbital weapons.
