The confirmed death toll from the late August flash flooding along the Nepal-China border has risen above 1,250, according to Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority. This is an increase from the toll of over 1,000 reported in early September. The disaster was triggered by a sudden glacial-related surge of water and mud crashing down river systems across the frontier, sweeping away entire communities and cutting off remote villages and hydropower construction sites.

Recovery and rescue operations have faced difficulties, particularly at hydropower sites where workers were trapped in flooded tunnels, requiring international teams with specialized drilling crews. Thousands of people remain unaccounted for, and officials have cautioned that the death toll will likely continue to rise as search teams reach isolated areas. Rescue crews are working through casualty records, debris fields, and collapsed buildings.

Hospitals and temples in Kathmandu serve as gathering points for grieving families searching for missing relatives. Nepali and Chinese authorities are coordinating across the border to reconcile casualty figures and identify victims. Meanwhile, international aid agencies are mobilizing relief supplies, though mountainous and hard-to-reach terrain continues to slow delivery to the worst-affected districts.
