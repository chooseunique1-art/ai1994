Five southern Indian states—Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Kerala—are increasing inter-state coordination to address federal challenges. Political leaders are considering a collective platform tentatively called a regional South Council, reviving strategies from the 1980s. 

Key grievances include upcoming population-based parliamentary constituency delimitation, which leaders argue penalizes states with successful family planning policies. Concerns also focus on tax devolution formulas, with leaders stating that southern states contribute higher federal revenue while receiving lower fiscal returns. Additionally, language policies and mining regulation amendments have increased friction with the central government. 

Karnataka's leadership invited neighboring chief ministers to aggregate regional data and present a unified petition, and even Andhra Pradesh, which is aligned with the ruling national coalition, highlighted demographic shifts and lower fertility rates. Proponents view the initiative as a defense of cooperative federalism, while opponents claim it risks amplifying a north-south political divide.
